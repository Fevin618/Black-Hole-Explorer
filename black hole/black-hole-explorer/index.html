Black Hole Categories

Terminology can vary in astronomy, but black holes are broadly categorized by their mass.

Stellar-mass

Mass: ~3 to 100 M☉

Formed by the gravitational collapse of a massive star at the end of its life.

Intermediate-mass

Mass: 102 to 105 M☉

Rarely observed, likely formed by the collision of smaller black holes.

Supermassive

Mass: 105 to 1010 M☉

Found at the center of almost all large galaxies (e.g., Sagittarius A*).

Gravitational Time Dilation

According to General Relativity, time passes slower closer to a massive object compared to a distant observer. This is known as gravitational time dilation.

Proper Time: The time measured by a clock moving with the observer.

Coordinate Time: The time measured by an observer infinitely far away from the gravitational field.

Educational Example:

If an observer were to safely hover at a distance of 1.5 times the Schwarzschild radius from the center of a non-rotating black hole, for every 1 hour that passes for them (proper time), roughly 1.73 hours would pass for a distant observer (coordinate time).

Note: This is a simplified example based on Schwarzschild spacetime. Calculating exactly what an observer experiences depends entirely on their trajectory and the specific spacetime geometry (e.g., whether the black hole is rotating).

Physics Concepts

Event Horizon
The boundary around a black hole beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape. It is not a physical surface, but a threshold in spacetime.
Singularity
The center of a black hole where matter is thought to be crushed to infinite density. Current physics (General Relativity) cannot fully describe this point; a theory of quantum gravity is needed.
Schwarzschild Radius
The radius of the event horizon for a non-rotating black hole. It is directly proportional to the mass of the black hole.
Accretion Disk
A structure formed by diffused material in orbital motion around a central body. Friction causes the disk to heat up and emit light and x-rays.
Gravitational Lensing
The bending of light caused by the intense gravitational field of a massive object, warping the visual appearance of background objects.
Spaghettification (Tidal Forces)
The extreme stretching of an object caused by the immense difference in gravitational pull across its length as it approaches a black hole.
Hawking Radiation
Theoretical thermal radiation predicted to be released by black holes due to quantum effects near the event horizon.
